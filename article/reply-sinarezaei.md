This is exactly the right question, and it's where I've tried to be most deliberate.

The contract I committed to is: **state is explicit data the agent owns, not ambient profile the browser owns.** Every session runs in a fresh webview with a non-persistent store (on macOS explicitly a `WKWebsiteDataStore` nonPersistent per session) — isolation is by construction, nothing hits disk. What survives a run is what the agent chose to export: `state_export` serializes the cookie jar as JSON, stored outside the engine, re-importable after a crash, a daemon restart, or an idle-release reap. Failure recovery falls out of that shape — a crashed WebKit helper loses nothing the agent can't re-import idempotently, and CI round-trips the cookie jar on all three engines.

The honest scope: the jar is **cookies only** today. And your instinct about the divergence is right — persistent profiles (cache + DOM storage + credentials on disk) bring engine-specific data schemas and state rot that differ across WebKit / WebView2 / WebKitGTK, so I've kept them out rather than inheriting the differences by accident. I've opened the roadmap discussion as an issue, including the "what does each engine actually persist by default" audit: https://github.com/slabbdev/navette/issues/6 — the options on the table are ephemeral-only, a jar that grows past cookies, or a deliberate named-session persistent profile. Would love your take there.
